### The primacy of consciousness

Some view consciousness plus time as a two-dimensional world, which is a fundamental proposition of idealism—consciousness is the primary element of the world.

The primacy of consciousness emphasizes its influence on matter. This is easy to understand: our decision to drink alcohol is a decision made by our consciousness, influencing the material world. In this scenario, consciousness is primary.

The most striking aspect of idealism is its emphasis on the uniqueness of consciousness. However, the world continues after death, making this a false proposition.

### World != consciousness

Idealism includes a viewpoint called "My mind is the world." This means that without me, the world ceases to exist; when I am gone, the world disappears. The extreme form of this viewpoint is called egocentrism.

Many people misunderstand "egocentrism" because it leads to extreme narrow-mindedness.

To some extent, "My mind is the world" is correct. Because when "I" die, the world ceases to have meaning.

「Après moi, le déluge」

However, a problem with egocentric action strategies is that the harm you do to the world will be repaid by the world. And sometimes this retribution comes very quickly.

I think the core of egocentrism is not emphasizing egocentrism, but rather emphasizing self-reliance and self-improvement.

Furthermore, "My mind is the world" is nonsense because social development is not determined by human will.

### The primacy of matter

The fundamental proposition of materialism is that the material world (the set of three-dimensional coordinates X, Y, Z) is the primary nature of the world.

The primacy of matter emphasizes its influence on consciousness. This is easily understood in a materialistic society—we drink alcohol, and intoxication leads to promiscuity. This is the influence of matter on our own consciousness.

However, I do not agree that matter is the sole nature of the world. Materialism cannot solve the problem of inner emptiness. The world of consciousness is an intangible yet definite existence. Just as people experience happiness and sadness, these emotions cannot be mechanically understood as matter.

### The world is not material

In a materialistic worldview, the soul doesn't exist. I find this argument quite uninteresting. Without a soul, we'd be nothing more than a bunch of walking corpses.

Leaving the soul aside, I think the two-dimensional world should also be time plus consciousness. For example, if we bring *Hamlet* into reality and act it out, then this "real world" is equivalent to the world of consciousness. The process of "acting" is actually a complete recreation of the world of consciousness.

The world of consciousness exists, but this existence requires comprehension. Without sufficient experience and memory, it cannot be understood.

### Summarize

In reality, if you watch TV series closely, you'll find that those two-dimensional images are human interpretations of reality and their preservation. That is to say, there is no primary order of importance between consciousness and matter. This is what is meant by "First hearing that song, I was still too young;Second hearing, I had become the very soul it mourned.
